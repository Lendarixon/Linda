Linda Probe vs dtSearch

dtSearch covers desktop search and broader collections. Probe is worth considering for a Windows file workflow with local OCR and search by meaning. This comparison relies on the competitor’s public site as of 30 October 2026.

Choose dtSearch if

You need search across folders, email attachments and databases, or web content through the built-in Spider. The Desktop / Network feature page lists these sources, highlighted hits and multiple search options. Check the edition for your deployment.

Choose Linda Probe if

Your task is finding files on your Windows PC, including scans or documents you remember by subject. Probe Pro adds local OCR, search by meaning and photo search by description or example. This comparison does not establish that Probe is faster or more accurate.

What Linda Probe does not offer

It is closed source and runs only on Windows 10 and 11. The free edition covers file names and exact words only, so OCR and search by meaning need a paid key. The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn. OCR can make mistakes on poor scans and handwriting.

Price

Linda Probe Free is for personal, non-commercial use. Probe Pro costs $10 once for one person, $30 once for up to 10 people, or $500 a year for an organization. Personal and Team include a full refund on request within 14 days.

The dtSearch store lists a $249 Desktop single-user licence for one person on up to two computers. Investigative use has separate terms.

We could not confirm built-in scan OCR or search by meaning on the dtSearch feature page linked below. Ask the vendor if these are requirements.
