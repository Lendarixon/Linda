01 — how it works

three detectors, one verdict

Three independent checks vote, and your text is reviewed part by part. You get one clear verdict, with the suspicious passages highlighted.

1

Paste or drop a file

Paste text or upload TXT, MD, DOCX or PDF. Files: up to 20 MB; PDFs: up to 500 pages. Longer texts take more time to analyse.

2

Get a verdict

AI, uncertain or human; the share of AI-looking text for mixed authorship; a colour map of the passages.

3

Choose: catch more, or accuse less

Sensitive finds more AI writing, but may wrongly flag a human text more often. Precise says “AI” only when two independent checks agree — fewer false alarms, but some AI writing slips through. For schools and publishers, where a false accusation hurts most, use Precise.

09 — questions

faq

What does the percentage in the result mean?

It is the confidence in the verdict shown next to it (“AI”, “human” or “uncertain”), not the share of the text written by AI: 88% next to “AI” means the models are fairly sure the text is machine-written. The share of the text that looks AI-written is shown separately (the colour map, “AI share” and the authorship bar). A high percentage is a reason to look closer, not proof; see “If the score is high”. Very short texts (under about 150 words) give less reliable results, and the app warns about it.

Are my texts uploaded anywhere?

No. Texts are analysed on your computer. The app connects to the internet only to download model files, to check for updates and, if you enter a key, to check that key (the key and a random device label are sent, nothing else).

Is it accurate? Can it accuse a human by mistake?

No detector is perfect. On held-out human texts the false-flag rate is about 0–2% for general writing, and 5.5% for TOEFL exam essays in sensitive mode (about 1% in precise mode, measured on version 1.3). Writers who are not native English speakers are affected more. Never use a result as the sole basis for decisions about people.

What about AI “humanizer” tools?

Version 1.3.1 uses new models merged from three trained variants. On a public humanizer test it flags 81% of rewritten texts (version 1.1: 70%, version 1.0: 31%). Humanizers change every month, so the models are updated regularly and the app downloads the updates.

Which languages?

English is the main language, but Russian and Polish now run on the same models. On our held-out test sets (350 AI and 350 human texts per language) the verdict “AI” catches about 80% (Polish) and 78% (Russian) of AI texts at 0.9% and 0.6% false alarms (version 1.1: 39% and 57%). Texts from newer models and rewritten texts are caught less often (Gemini-rewritten Polish: 48%, Russian: 50%). The sets are small, expect ±5 points. We have not measured Russian or Polish against other detectors, so we make no comparison claim. Other languages are not supported. Exact figures are in the results section.

Is it free?

Yes — for personal non-commercial use: your own texts, study, hobby, non-commercial research. No registration, no key, no limits. A paid key is only the permission for commercial use (paid work, clients, companies, schools and universities, products): it hides the licence reminder; the app itself works the same without it.
