01 — how it works

three detectors, one verdict

Three independent checks vote, and your text is reviewed part by part. You get one clear verdict, with the suspicious passages highlighted.

1

Paste or drop a file

Text, .docx, .pdf or .md — no length limit (the longer the text, the longer the check).

2

Get a verdict

AI, uncertain or human; the share of AI-looking text for mixed authorship; a colour map of the passages.

3

Choose: catch more, or accuse less

Sensitive finds more AI writing, but may wrongly flag a human text more often. Precise says “AI” only when two independent checks agree — fewer false alarms, but some AI writing slips through. For schools and publishers, where a false accusation hurts most, use Precise.

09 — questions

faq

Are my texts uploaded anywhere?

No. Texts are analysed on your computer. The app connects to the internet only to download model files, to check for updates and, if you enter a key, to check that key (the key and a random device label are sent, nothing else).

Is it accurate? Can it accuse a human by mistake?

No detector is perfect. On held-out human texts the false-flag rate is about 0–2% for general writing, and 4.4% for TOEFL exam essays in sensitive mode (0% in precise mode). Writers who are not native English speakers are affected more. Never use a result as the sole basis for decisions about people.

What about AI “humanizer” tools?

Version 1.1 was retrained on real humanizer outputs. On a public humanizer test it flags 70% of rewritten texts (version 1.0: 31%). Humanizers change every month, so the models are updated regularly and the app downloads the updates.

Which languages?

English is the main language: everything is tuned and measured on English texts. Russian and Polish work at moderate quality: the app also learned on Russian and Polish texts. On our test sets (150 AI and 150 human texts per language) it ranks AI writing above human writing well — 98% for Russian, 99% for Polish — but the “AI” line is drawn for English, so the verdict “AI” catches about 63% (Russian) and 51% (Polish) of AI texts with 0–1% false alarms; the “uncertain” band catches more. Treat Russian and Polish results as a hint, not a verdict. We have not measured Russian or Polish against other detectors, so we make no comparison claim. Other languages are not supported. Exact figures are also in the results section.

Is it free?

Yes — for personal non-commercial use: your own texts, study, hobby, non-commercial research. No registration, no key, no limits. A paid key is only the permission for commercial use (paid work, clients, companies, schools and universities, products): it hides the licence reminder; the app itself works the same without it.
